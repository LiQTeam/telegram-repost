# Link Building Playbooks

Five proven, actionable link building strategies with step-by-step processes, outreach templates, and success benchmarks.

---

## Playbook 1: Skyscraper Technique

**Goal:** Find top-performing content in your niche, create a substantially better version, then outreach to sites linking to the original.

**Best for:** B2B companies, SaaS, niche blogs, educational content sites.
**Expected success rate:** 5-12% response rate, 3-8% link conversion rate.
**Time investment:** 15-25 hours per campaign (content creation + outreach).

### Step-by-Step Process

1. **Identify link-worthy topics.** Use `analyze_backlinks` on competitor URLs to find their most-linked content. Look for pages with 50+ referring domains -- these topics have proven link demand.

2. **Analyze the top-ranking content.** Read the top 5-10 results for your target keyword. Note gaps: outdated statistics, missing sections, poor design, lack of visuals, insufficient depth.

3. **Create a substantially better version.** Your content must be clearly superior in at least 2-3 ways:
   - More comprehensive (cover subtopics others miss)
   - More current (fresh data, updated for the current year)
   - Better designed (custom graphics, interactive elements, better UX)
   - More actionable (templates, tools, step-by-step walkthroughs)
   - Better sourced (primary research, expert quotes, original data)

4. **Build a prospect list.** Export the backlink profiles of the top-ranking pages. Collect all domains linking to them. Remove duplicates, irrelevant sites, and domains with DR below 20. Target: 100-300 prospects.

5. **Find contact information.** For each prospect, find the author or editor email. Use the site's contact page, LinkedIn, or email finder tools. Avoid generic info@ addresses when possible.

6. **Send personalized outreach.** Reference their specific link, explain why your content is a better resource, and make the ask clear.

7. **Follow up.** Send one follow-up 5-7 days after the initial email. Do not send more than one follow-up.

8. **Track and iterate.** Log every response. Analyze which angles and subject lines perform best. Refine your approach for the next campaign.

### Outreach Template 1: Initial Email

```
Subject: Better resource for your [topic] page

Hi [Name],

I was reading your article on [their article title] and noticed you
linked to [competitor's content title] as a resource on [topic].

I recently published an updated guide on the same topic that covers
[2-3 specific improvements: e.g., "2026 statistics, a comparison
framework, and downloadable templates"] that the original resource
doesn't include.

Here it is: [your URL]

If you find it useful, it might be a worthwhile replacement or
addition for your readers.

Either way, enjoyed your article -- especially the section on
[specific detail from their content].

Best,
[Your name]
```

### Outreach Template 2: Follow-Up

```
Subject: Re: Better resource for your [topic] page

Hi [Name],

Just bumping this up in case it got buried. I shared an updated
[topic] guide that might be a useful addition to your [their article
title] article.

No worries if it's not a fit -- I appreciate your time.

[Your name]
```

## Playbook 2: Digital PR / Original Data Studies

**Goal:** Create original research or data analysis, then pitch it to journalists and publications for high-authority coverage and backlinks.

**Best for:** SaaS companies with proprietary data, research firms, companies in data-rich industries (finance, real estate, health, tech).
**Expected success rate:** 3-10% journalist response rate; a single successful campaign can yield 20-100+ links.
**Time investment:** 30-50 hours per campaign (research + content + pitching).

### Step-by-Step Process

1. **Identify a newsworthy angle.** Think about what data you have access to that others do not. Look for:
   - Industry trends (year-over-year changes)
   - Consumer behavior data (surveys, usage patterns)
   - Geographic or demographic comparisons
   - Counterintuitive findings that challenge assumptions
   - Timely topics tied to current news cycles or seasons

2. **Collect and analyze the data.** Use your internal data, run a survey (1,000+ respondents for credibility), or analyze publicly available datasets in a novel way. Ensure statistical rigor -- journalists will scrutinize methodology.

3. **Create the content asset.** Build a dedicated landing page or blog post with:
   - Key findings summary (5-10 bullet points)
   - Custom charts and data visualizations (shareable, embeddable)
   - Full methodology section
   - Downloadable data or press kit
   - Expert commentary or quotes

4. **Build a targeted media list.** Identify 50-150 journalists who have covered similar topics in the past 6 months. Use Twitter/X, publication mastheads, and journalist databases. Segment by publication tier (Tier 1: national, Tier 2: industry, Tier 3: niche blogs).

5. **Craft a concise pitch.** Lead with the most compelling finding. Keep the pitch under 200 words. Include one key data point in the subject line.

6. **Send personalized pitches.** Reference the journalist's past work. Explain why their audience would care. Offer exclusive data or an expert interview.

7. **Provide assets on request.** Have high-resolution charts, raw data tables, and expert quotes ready to send within hours.

8. **Amplify coverage.** Once published, share the coverage on social media, in newsletters, and on your site. This secondary amplification often triggers additional organic links.

### Outreach Template 1: Journalist Pitch

```
Subject: New data: [Key finding in 8 words or fewer]

Hi [Name],

I read your recent piece on [their article] -- the section on
[specific detail] was particularly insightful.

We just completed a study of [sample size / data source] that
found [one compelling, specific finding with a number].

A few other highlights:
- [Finding 2 with specific data point]
- [Finding 3 with specific data point]

The full study with methodology and visualizations is here:
[URL]

Happy to provide the raw data, additional analysis, or connect
you with our [title] for an expert quote if this is something
your readers would find valuable.

Best,
[Your name]
[Title, Company]
```

### Outreach Template 2: Exclusive Offer

```
Subject: Exclusive data for [Publication Name]: [topic]

Hi [Name],

Would [Publication Name] be interested in an exclusive first look
at our new [topic] study?

The key finding: [one-sentence summary with a data point].

We surveyed [sample size] and the results challenge the common
assumption that [conventional wisdom]. I can send the full data
set and visualizations today if you'd like first access before
we publish broadly.

Let me know if you're interested -- happy to work on your timeline.

[Your name]
```

## Playbook 3: Broken Link Building

**Goal:** Find broken outbound links on relevant websites, then offer your content as a working replacement.

**Best for:** Any niche, particularly effective in industries with high content turnover (tech, news, health). Works well for resource-heavy sites.
**Expected success rate:** 5-15% link conversion rate (higher than cold outreach because you are solving a problem for the webmaster).
**Time investment:** 8-15 hours per campaign.

### Step-by-Step Process

1. **Identify target pages.** Find resource pages, "best of" lists, and link-heavy guides in your niche. Search Google for:
   ```
   [your keyword] + "resources"
   [your keyword] + "useful links"
   [your keyword] + "recommended reading"
   [your keyword] + intitle:"links"
   ```

2. **Scan for broken outbound links.** Use a browser extension (Check My Links, Broken Link Checker) or crawling tool to scan each target page for 404 errors on outbound links. Record the broken URL, the anchor text, and the context.

3. **Verify the broken links.** Confirm each 404 is truly dead (not a temporary error). Check the Wayback Machine to understand what the original content covered.

4. **Match or create replacement content.** If you have existing content that covers the same topic as the broken link, use it. If not, create a new piece that matches the intent and scope of the dead page.

5. **Find the right contact.** Identify the page author or site editor. Avoid contact forms when possible -- direct email to the decision-maker is more effective.

6. **Send a helpful outreach email.** Position yourself as helping them fix a problem, not asking for a favor. Mention the specific broken link, where it appears, and offer your content as a replacement.

7. **Follow up once.** A single follow-up 5-7 days later. Keep it short.

### Outreach Template 1: Broken Link Notification

```
Subject: Broken link on your [topic] page

Hi [Name],

I was reading your excellent [page title / resource page] and
noticed that the link to [broken link anchor text / original
site name] appears to be broken (returns a 404).

I recently published a guide on the same topic that covers
[brief description of your content]:

[Your URL]

It might work as a replacement if you're updating that page.
Either way, wanted to give you a heads-up about the dead link.

Thanks for putting together such a great resource.

[Your name]
```

### Outreach Template 2: Multiple Broken Links

```
Subject: Found a few broken links on your [topic] resources page

Hi [Name],

I was going through your [page title] and found a few links
that seem to be broken:

- [Anchor text 1] -> returns 404
- [Anchor text 2] -> domain expired
- [Anchor text 3] -> returns 404

For the [anchor text 1] reference, I have a resource that covers
the same topic: [Your URL]

Thought you'd want to know so you can update the page for your
readers.

Best,
[Your name]
```

## Playbook 4: Resource Page Outreach

**Goal:** Find curated resource pages, "best tools" lists, and link directories in your niche, then request inclusion.

**Best for:** Tools, software, educational resources, non-profits, local businesses (local resource pages). Works across all industries.
**Expected success rate:** 8-20% (the highest of all outreach strategies, because resource pages exist specifically to link out).
**Time investment:** 5-10 hours per campaign.

### Step-by-Step Process

1. **Find resource pages in your niche.** Use Google search operators:
   ```
   [your niche] + "resources"
   [your niche] + "useful links"
   [your niche] + "recommended tools"
   [your niche] + "best [product/service] list"
   [your niche] + intitle:"resources" inurl:resources
   [your niche] + intitle:"links" inurl:links
   ```

2. **Qualify each resource page.** Filter for:
   - DR 20+ (enough authority to pass value)
   - Actually maintained and updated (check last modified date)
   - Relevant to your specific niche or subtopic
   - Has a real audience (not a thin page created solely for SEO)

3. **Audit existing listings.** Review what is already listed on the page. Understand the inclusion criteria -- what types of resources are featured? What quality standard do they maintain?

4. **Prepare your pitch angle.** Identify what makes your resource uniquely valuable compared to what is already listed. Find a gap you can fill.

5. **Find the page owner's email.** Check the about page, author bio, or LinkedIn. For university or organization pages, look for the department contact.

6. **Send a concise, specific email.** Name the exact page, explain why your resource fits, and make the ask clear.

7. **Follow up once** after 7 days if no response.

### Outreach Template 1: Standard Request

```
Subject: Suggestion for your [niche] resources page

Hi [Name],

I came across your [page title] page and found it really helpful --
I bookmarked several of the resources you listed.

I noticed you don't currently have a resource for [specific gap
or subtopic]. I recently created [brief description of your
resource and what makes it valuable]:

[Your URL]

Would you consider adding it to the list? I think it would be a
valuable addition for your readers who are looking for [specific
benefit].

Thanks for maintaining such a great resource.

[Your name]
```

### Outreach Template 2: Educational / .edu Resource Pages

```
Subject: Free [resource type] for your [department/topic] page

Hi [Name],

I'm reaching out because I saw your department's resource page
for [topic] students: [their URL]

We've created a free [describe your resource: tool, guide,
dataset, course material] that several universities have found
useful for [specific use case]:

[Your URL]

It's completely free and regularly updated. Would it be a good
fit for your resources page?

Happy to provide any additional information.

Best regards,
[Your name]
[Title, Organization]
```

## Playbook 5: HARO / Journalist Response

**Goal:** Monitor journalist queries and provide expert quotes, data, or insights in exchange for backlinks from high-authority publications.

**Best for:** Founders, executives, subject matter experts, agencies, consultants. Any industry where you can provide genuine expertise.
**Expected success rate:** 2-10% of pitches result in placement. A single placement can yield a DR 70-90+ backlink.
**Time investment:** 30-60 minutes per day monitoring and responding. Low per-pitch effort but requires consistency.

### Step-by-Step Process

1. **Sign up for journalist query platforms.** Register for:
   - HARO (Help a Reporter Out) -- now part of Connectively
   - Qwoted
   - SourceBottle
   - Terkel
   - Featured.com
   - Twitter/X #journorequest hashtag
   - ProfNet

2. **Set up keyword alerts.** Configure notifications for your areas of expertise. Be specific -- broad alerts generate too much noise. Example: instead of "marketing," set alerts for "SEO," "content marketing," "link building."

3. **Respond within 2 hours.** Journalists work on tight deadlines. The first 5-10 quality responses usually get selected. Speed is critical.

4. **Craft a concise, expert response.** Structure your pitch:
   - One-line introduction (who you are, why you're qualified)
   - Direct answer to their question (2-4 sentences, quotable)
   - One supporting data point or example
   - One unique insight or contrarian take
   - Your credentials (title, company, relevant experience)

5. **Include a headshot and bio.** Journalists often need author photos and bios for the piece. Have a professional headshot and 2-3 sentence bio ready to send immediately.

6. **Provide more than asked.** If they ask for one quote, offer two different angles. If they want data, include a chart. The easier you make the journalist's job, the more likely you are to be featured.

7. **Follow up only if they request additional info.** Do not follow up on HARO pitches -- journalists are inundated and will contact you if interested.

8. **Build long-term journalist relationships.** When you are featured, thank the journalist. Share their article. Become a reliable source they come back to repeatedly.

### Response Template 1: Expert Quote

```
Subject: RE: [Exact query subject line] -- [Your Name], [Title]

Hi [Journalist Name],

[One sentence connecting your expertise to their query.]

Here's my response to your question:

"[2-4 sentence quotable response. Be specific, use numbers
where possible, and provide a unique perspective that adds
value beyond the obvious answer.]"

For context, [1-2 sentences providing a supporting example,
data point, or case study from your experience].

About me: [Your Name] is the [Title] at [Company], where
[1 sentence about relevant experience]. [He/She/They] has
[relevant credential: years of experience, publications,
clients served, etc.].

Headshot attached. Happy to provide additional quotes or
data if needed.

Best,
[Your Name]
[Title], [Company]
[LinkedIn URL]
[Phone number for quick follow-up]
```

### Response Template 2: Data-Backed Response

```
Subject: RE: [Query subject line] -- Data from [Company]

Hi [Journalist Name],

I can provide some relevant data for your piece on [topic].

Key data point: [Specific statistic from your data, e.g.,
"Our analysis of 10,000 websites found that 67% of small
businesses have at least one critical technical SEO issue."]

Additional findings:
- [Data point 2]
- [Data point 3]

Quote for attribution: "[2-3 sentence expert interpretation
of what this data means for your audience.]"

-- [Your Name], [Title] at [Company]

I can send the full dataset or methodology if helpful. Our
study is published here: [URL]

Best,
[Your Name]
```

## Playbook Success Tracking

### Metrics to Track Per Campaign

| Metric | What to Measure |
|--------|-----------------|
| Prospects contacted | Total outreach emails sent |
| Response rate | % of prospects who replied (any response) |
| Link conversion rate | % of prospects who added a link |
| Average DR of acquired links | Quality benchmark for the campaign |
| Links acquired | Total new backlinks from the campaign |
| Cost per link | (Hours spent * hourly rate) / links acquired |
| Referral traffic | Actual visitors from acquired links |

### Benchmark Conversion Rates

| Playbook | Typical Response Rate | Typical Link Rate |
|----------|----------------------|-------------------|
| Skyscraper | 8-15% | 3-8% |
| Digital PR | 3-10% | Varies (1 story = 20-100 links) |
| Broken Link Building | 10-20% | 5-15% |
| Resource Page Outreach | 15-25% | 8-20% |
| HARO / Journalist | 5-15% | 2-10% per pitch |

### When to Pivot

- If response rate drops below 3% after 100 emails, rethink the angle or content quality
- If you are getting responses but not links, the content may not match expectations -- ask for feedback
- If conversion is high but DR is low, shift to higher-authority targets even if it means lower volume
- If a playbook consistently underperforms benchmarks after 3 campaigns, try a different strategy
